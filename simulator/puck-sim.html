Pick a mode on the left to see it on the puck and change what it does. Each turn and press can send a keyboard shortcut, go through the companion app on your computer, or reach a service like Spotify or Home Assistant. Your changes stay in this browser. Use Copy settings to export them as a lineup for the puck (firmware/tools/send_modes.py).

The 1.28″ knob display, the current target. Screens are drawn at its real 240 × 240 pixels and about 12% smaller than the 1.46″. Text is kept at 11 px or larger, so labels that get cut off with … are the spots that need a redesign for the small screen.

Drag the knurled ring or scroll over the puck to turn it. Click the screen to press; hold it to open the mode menu on the puck. Keys: ← → turn, Enter press (twice quickly for a double press, or D), H hold.

What the puck sends

Each event and the route it takes. Newest first.

    How the dial follows your screen

    The puck can't see your monitor, so a small companion app on your computer checks which window is in front and tells the puck. The puck draws that mode's screen and sends back every turn and press. Some actions need no computer at all: keyboard shortcuts go straight over USB, and Wi-Fi services like Home Assistant are called by the puck itself.

    Companion appTray app on Windows or macOS. Watches the front window, mutes the mic, and talks to services that need your account, like Spotify, Slack and OBS.
    USB ⇄
    USB linkThe ESP32-S3 appears as a serial port for messages and as a keyboard with media keys, so shortcuts and volume work with no app installed.
    ⇄ dial
    Puck firmwareReads the dial, draws screens with LVGL, and keeps each mode's settings. It can also reach Wi-Fi services directly.
